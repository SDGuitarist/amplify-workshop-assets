# Sound & AV Prep Checklist — Expressive Arts San Diego

**Why this matters:** Research shows poor audio drops perceived speaker credibility by 19% and information retention by 30%, even when the content is identical. Audio quality diverts up to 40% of listener mental resources to processing instead of learning. Alex's sound background is a genuine competitive advantage here.

---

## Pre-Event (Before April 25)

- [ ] **Visit Expressive Arts for acoustics check** — Bring a Bluetooth speaker or small PA. Play spoken word audio from different spots in the room. Walk the room and listen for dead spots, echo, and reflections. Note room dimensions, ceiling height, hard vs. soft surfaces.
- [ ] **Test projector audio** — If using the venue's projector/screen setup, check if the built-in speakers are sufficient for 30 people. They usually aren't.
- [ ] **Decide: venue audio or bring your own** — Based on the visit, decide whether to bring a portable PA (e.g., JBL Eon One, Bose S1 Pro, or similar) for voice reinforcement during the demo sections.
- [ ] **Mic check** — If the room is larger than ~25x25 ft, a wireless lapel mic frees Alex to move during demos. Test range and battery life.
- [ ] **Laptop audio out** — Confirm the projector or external speaker accepts 3.5mm or Bluetooth from Alex's laptop for the live demo sections.

## Day-Of Setup (April 25, arrive by 9:00 AM)

- [ ] Sound check with someone standing in the back row
- [ ] Test laptop → projector → audio chain end-to-end with a demo clip
- [ ] Set volume for speaking voice (not music — speech intelligibility is different)
- [ ] Position speaker(s) so sound reaches back of room without blasting front row
- [ ] Have a backup plan: if audio fails, move seating closer and project voice

## Backup Plan

If venue audio is inadequate and Alex can't bring gear:
- Reduce seating depth (semicircle or U-shape, max 4 rows)
- Project voice (Alex has decades of stage experience)
- Keep demo audio at laptop level and have front-row participants confirm they can hear

## Why This Is a Differentiator

Most workshop facilitators plug a laptop into a projector and hope for the best. Alex has 30+ years of professional audio experience. Showing up with clean sound in a 30-person room is an invisible quality signal that sets the baseline for everything else. Participants won't notice good audio, but they will absolutely notice bad audio. This is a "zero-cost credibility signal" — it costs nothing extra but shapes the entire perception of the event.
