# Workshop Talking Points — Research-Backed Stats

Stats Alex can drop naturally during the workshop. Mapped to specific phases.
Source: Two 2026 research reports (Sold-Out Event + Enterprise AI Event Management).

---

## Phase 1 — OPEN

### Slop Hook (10:05)
After showing the flat vs. expert-led outputs:
> "There's actually a word for this now. When AI produces generic output because nobody's leading it, people call it slop. 82% of people react negatively when they encounter it. Mentions of that word went up 200% last year. Your clients can feel it even when they can't name it."

### Capacity Reframe (10:19)
After describing the bottleneck:
> "Here's something that surprised me. 96% of organizations still require a human to approve what AI produces. Only 4% let it act on its own. That's not a limitation of the technology. That's the design. The human in the loop is the whole point."

### AI Anxiety → AI Agency (10:24)
When naming the fear in the room:
> "64% of people use AI tools regularly now. Your clients are using them. Your competitors are using them. The question isn't whether to engage with it. The question is whether you're going to lead it or let it flatten you."

---

## Phase 3 — ORIENT

### Assistants vs. Agents (10:55)
When making the distinction:
> "Only 4% of teams let AI run without human approval. The rest use it exactly the way we're about to. You set the rules. You define the standards. The AI executes within those boundaries."

---

## Phase 4 — DEMONSTRATE

### Pipeline Architecture (11:20)
When showing how the system works:
> "73% of buyers say they value transparency above everything else. They can tell when a response is real versus generated. That's why every claim in this system has to trace back to something verifiable. The QA gate catches anything that doesn't."

### Flat vs. Expert-Led Demo (11:30)
After showing both outputs side by side:
> "The research on this is clear. When people encounter generic AI content, 82% react negatively. But when expertise is in the system, the output doesn't just sound better. It earns trust. Because there's a real person behind it."

---

## Phase 5 — EXPERIENCE

### Permission Check-In (12:45)
When normalizing the discomfort:
> "Even the biggest companies in the world haven't gone fully autonomous with AI. 96% require human sign-off. You being careful with this isn't being slow. It's being professional. The graduated trust model is how every serious organization approaches this."

---

## Phase 6 — INTEGRATE

### Naming Dual Literacy (1:15)
When making the case for both literacies:
> "64% of your potential clients use AI daily. They know what generic output looks like. That's the AI fluency side — understanding what the tools can do. But knowing what only YOU can do — that's the human literacy side. And that's what makes the output worth sending."

---

## Phase 7 — CLOSE

### Floor and Ceiling (1:30)
When delivering the intellectual close:
> "The research calls it content commoditization. When everyone has access to AI, the floor comes up. Anyone can sound competent. But research also shows that 82% of people have negative reactions to generic AI content. The ceiling went up too. Genuine expertise, real human attention — that's premium now. That's what you just built today."

## Usage Notes

- Don't read stats from notes. Memorize the 3-4 you'll use most and let them come up naturally.
- The strongest ones for this audience: 82% slop, 96% human-in-loop, 64% use AI daily.
- Audio stats (19%/30%) are for Alex's own prep motivation, not for the room.
- Never stack stats. One per phase transition, maximum.
- If a stat doesn't land naturally in the moment, skip it. The frameworks carry the session. Stats are seasoning.
