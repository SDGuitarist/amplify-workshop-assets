# Brand Voice — Alex Guillen / Amplify Workshop

## Who Alex Is (For Voice Calibration)

Working musician with 30+ years of performance and 2,500+ events. Berklee College of Music degree. Runs Pacific Flow Entertainment, a Latin music consultancy in San Diego. Built his entire business development operation on AI systems he created himself. Now teaching what he's built.

He's not a tech influencer. He's not an AI evangelist. He's a working creative who figured something out and is sharing it with peers.

---

## Voice DNA

**Register:** Peer-to-peer. Alex talks like a colleague who's a few steps ahead, not a guru looking down. Warm but direct. Confident without being salesy.

**Rhythm:** Short sentences mixed with longer ones. Fragments are fine. Dashes over semicolons. Casual punctuation. Not sloppy, just human.

**Energy:** Grounded enthusiasm. He's genuinely excited about what he's built, but he doesn't oversell. He leads with what it's done for him, not what it'll do for you.

**Closes:** Direct but not pushy. "You in?" / "Want me to save you a spot?" / "Let me know if you're in." Never begging. Assumes mutual respect.

## Platform Voice Adjustments

### Instagram
- Hook-first. Lead with a question or a bold statement in the first line.
- Conversational, slightly punchy. Shorter paragraphs.
- Hashtags at the end, not woven into copy.
- Close with "link in bio" or a direct CTA.
- Hashtags: 15-20. Mix broad discovery (#AIworkshop #CreativeBusiness), niche (#SanDiegoCreatives #HumanLedAI), and branded (#AmplifyWorkshop).

### Facebook
- Personal narrative voice. "I've been building something" energy.
- Can be longer. People read on Facebook if the opening pulls them in.
- End with a share ask: "Know someone who'd benefit? Send this their way."
- Warmer, more storytelling-oriented than Instagram.
- Hashtags: Skip them. Hashtags don't drive discovery on Facebook. They just clutter the post.

### LinkedIn
- Thought leadership angle. Lead with the Wedge ("work deeper, not faster").
- Professional register but still Alex. Not corporate, not stiff.
- Can reference the market gap (tech voices vs. traditional creatives).
- Hashtags: 3-5 max. Professional, discoverable. Too many looks spammy on LinkedIn.
- More framework language is acceptable here.

## Drafting Principles (Non-Negotiable)

1. **Lead with results, not pitched benefits.** Share what it's done for Alex. Don't promise outcomes for others.
2. **Never fabricate.** No invented stats, no made-up testimonials, no claims Alex can't back up.
3. **Don't call people beginners.** Say "designed for any level" or "we go through everything step-by-step together."
4. **Don't re-sell people who've already said yes.** If someone's committed, confirm and move forward.
5. **Connect to their specific situation** when possible. Generic posts are fine for broad reach, but personalized DMs should reference what you know about the person.
6. **Direct close.** Always end with a clear next step.

## Positioning Language (Approved for Use)

**Workshop title:** Amplify: The Power of Human-Led AI
**Marketing hook:** "What happens when expertise leads AI?"
**Core phrase:** "Human-Led AI"
**Primary tagline:** "AI that assists, not replaces"

**Counter-narrative (use sparingly, high-impact moments):**
AI raises the floor, which makes the ceiling matter more. When anyone can generate content, real creativity stands out. Don't compete with AI on efficiency. Compete on humanity.

## What NOT to Sound Like

- Generic AI hype ("10x your productivity!" / "The future is here!")
- Corporate webinar copy ("Join us for an exciting opportunity to...")
- Fear-based marketing ("You'll be left behind if you don't...")
- Condescending tech-bro ("It's actually really simple once you understand...")
- Overly polished marketing speak (Alex sounds like a person, not a brand)

---

## Hard Language Rules

**No em-dashes.** Never use them in any output. Use periods, commas, or line breaks instead. Alex's natural writing uses short sentences and fragments, not long clauses joined by dashes.

**No overused AI vocabulary.** The following words and phrases are banned from all copy:
- "Unlock" / "Unleash" / "Supercharge" / "Turbocharge"
- "Game-changing" / "Revolutionary" / "Cutting-edge"
- "Leverage" (use "use" instead)
- "Harness the power of"
- "Deep dive" / "Double down"
- "Seamless" / "Seamlessly"
- "Elevate" (except in the workshop title context)
- "Journey" (when describing a workshop or product)
- "Transform your" + [noun] as a marketing formula
- "In today's fast-paced world" or any variation
- "Navigate the landscape"
- "Empower" / "Empowering"
- "Delve" / "Delve into"
- "Robust" / "Comprehensive" / "Holistic"
- "Passionate about"
- "It's not just about X, it's about Y" (overused structure)

**No hype punctuation.** Limit exclamation marks to one per post maximum. Zero is better. Alex's energy comes through word choice, not punctuation.

**Confidence calibration.** Confident about experience and work ethic. Never confident about perfection or guaranteed outcomes. That's the line. The test: would Alex say this sentence to a friend at a bar? "I know exactly what to say to every client" = no. "I've been reading rooms for 30 years" = yes. Confidence in experience is Alex. Confidence in perfection is not.

**When in doubt, read it out loud.** If it sounds like a LinkedIn influencer or a Canva template, rewrite it. If it sounds like something Alex would say standing in a room with a friend, it's right.
