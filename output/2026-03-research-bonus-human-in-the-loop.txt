Everyone thinks AI is taking over. The data says otherwise.

96% of organizations still require human approval for AI actions. Only 4% let agents run without someone signing off.

That's not because the technology can't do more. It's because the results are better when a person who understands the domain is in the loop.

I see this in my own systems every day. My lead responder doesn't decide what to send. I decided that when I built it. The AI executes my judgment at speed. But the judgment is mine. The voice is mine. The standards are mine.

The phrase for this is "human-in-the-loop." It sounds technical. It's not. It means: you built the system, you set the rules, and you approve what goes out. The AI handles the repetition. You handle the thinking.

That's the model we work through on April 25. Not "let AI do everything." Not "be afraid of AI." Something more practical: build AI around what you already know, and stay in the loop where it matters.

If you're running a business on your expertise and you want to keep it that way while still using these tools, that's exactly what this is for.

What's the one part of your workflow where you'd want AI to handle the repetition but you'd never hand over the judgment?
