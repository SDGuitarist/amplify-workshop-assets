The prompt isn't the art. Your expertise is the art. The prompt is creative direction.

Here's a stat that surprised me. Only 4% of organizations let AI act without human approval. 96% still require a person in the loop.

That's not because the technology can't do more. It's because the results are better when someone who knows the domain is steering.

I see the same thing in my own systems. When I write a flat prompt, I get a flat response. When I bring 30 years of reading rooms, qualifying leads, and knowing what my clients actually need, the AI produces something I'd actually send.

The method is simple:

State your credential. Not to impress the AI. To give it context.
Define the nuance. What does a generic response get wrong in your field?
Command the synthesis. Tell it to use your philosophy, your voice, your standards.

That's what I call Expert-First prompting. It's the core exercise at the workshop on April 25. Participants run two prompts side by side. Same AI. Same task. The only difference is whether their expertise is in the prompt or not.

The gap between those two outputs is the whole argument for human-led AI.

What's the one thing about your work that AI consistently gets wrong when you don't specify it?
