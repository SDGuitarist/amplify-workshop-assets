I've been thinking about something that keeps coming up in conversations about AI.

People ask me what I teach at the workshop, and when I say "AI literacy," they nod like they know what that means. But most people think it means learning how to use the tools. How to write prompts. How to get ChatGPT to do things.

That's half of it. Maybe less than half.

The other part is the stuff you already know. Your judgment. Your taste. The instinct that tells you when something looks right on paper but feels wrong. The experience that lets you read between the lines of what a client is actually asking for.

AI fluency without that human side is just automation. You're producing more, but more of what? I spent my first few months with AI doing exactly that. Cranking out emails and proposals that were technically fine and completely forgettable. They could've come from anyone.

The shift happened when I stopped trying to learn AI and started teaching AI what I know. My pricing instincts. How I qualify a lead in thirty seconds. The difference between what a client says they want and what they actually need. That's 30 years of work, and it turns out AI can run on it, but only if you put it in.

Both skills together is where it gets interesting. You need to know what AI can do. And you need to know what you bring that AI can't replicate. The workshop on April 25 is built around that intersection. Not just the tools. The thinking behind how you use them.
