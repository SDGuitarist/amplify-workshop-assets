Phase 2 — Diagnose

Participants connect the workshop to their own business before they touch a keyboard.
Drafted
The Three Questions — Participant Diagnostic
1
Where are you irreplaceable?
What do you do that requires your specific judgment, relationships, or creative instinct?
2
Where are you just doing tasks?
What operational work consumes your time without leveraging what makes you exceptional?
3
If you had those task-hours back — what would you actually do with them?
This is the desire question. It reveals motivation.
Format
1
4 min silent writing. On paper, not laptops. Personal reflection.
2
Cross-category pair share. Photographer with caterer, designer with consultant. Brief share: Q1 and Q3.
3
Room collection. Alex gathers a few answers. These become live material for the rest of the session.

Expert-First Exercise
1
Flat prompt. "Write a response to a client inquiry about [their service]." 2 min.
2
State your credential. "I have [X] years doing [Y]. My approach is [Z]."
3
Define the nuance. "Generic responses fail because [specific reason]."
4
Command the synthesis. Rewrite and run. 4 min.
Done Tested timing. Works in 15 min with room share.
Assistants vs. Agents
Assistant Mode

"Help me write this email." Conversational. On demand. Useful but limited.

Agent Mode

"Every lead: extract facts, classify, draft response, check 9 criteria, tell me if ready to send."

Done This sets up the live pipeline demo in Phase 4.

Phase 6 — Integrate

Name the framework underneath everything they've done. The implicit becomes explicit.
Drafting
The 5 I's — The Reveal
"Everything you did today — from the Three Questions to the Expert-First prompt to evaluating your own output — you were following a framework the whole time. You just didn't have a name for it."
— 5 I's introduction · 1:00
I
Intent
"When you answered 'where are you irreplaceable,' you were defining Intent."
I
Identity
"When you stated your credential in Expert-First prompting, you were protecting Identity."
I
Inference
"When you moved from a flat prompt to a rich one, that was Inference."
I
Iteration
"When your output didn't pass the five questions and you revised your prompt — that was Iteration. Not failure. Craft."
I
Integrity
"When you asked 'can I actually prove this?' and removed what you couldn't — that was Integrity."
Done Each I ties directly to an exercise they already completed. Reveal, not lecture.
Naming Dual Literacy
"AI fluency without human literacy produces slop. Human literacy without AI fluency leaves you doing everything manually. Both together? That's what we built today. And that's what gives you agency."
— Dual Literacy thesis · 1:15
