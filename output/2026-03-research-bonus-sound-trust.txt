Research shows that poor audio drops a speaker's perceived credibility by 19%.

Information retention drops 30%. Same content. Same speaker. Same words. But when the sound quality is bad, the audience rates the speaker as less intelligent, less trustworthy, and less likeable.

I've been a professional musician for over 30 years. I've played 2,500+ events. And the one thing I've learned about live performance that most people never think about is this: the audience doesn't separate the message from the medium. If the sound is bad, the content feels bad. If the sound is clean, the content feels credible. It's invisible when it's right. It's the only thing you notice when it's wrong.

That same discipline goes into how I'm building this workshop. Not because anyone asked me to test the room acoustics at Expressive Arts San Diego. Because it's what I do. It's how I think about every room I've ever walked into.

On April 25, 30 people are going to learn how to lead AI with their expertise. And the room is going to sound exactly right. Because that's what "human-led" means. The details that only a human would care about.
