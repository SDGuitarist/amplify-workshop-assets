Something clicked for me this week and I want to share it.

I write a lot for my business. Emails, proposals, follow-ups. And I'm good at it when I have the time to sit down and think. The problem is I don't always have the time. So some messages get my best thinking and some get whatever I can fire off between meetings.

I sat down with a writing tool this week and tried to fix that. I taught it how I actually write. Not "write well." How I write. The rhythms I fall into. How I open differently depending on who I'm talking to. How I know when to be brief and when someone needs the full picture.

It picked up my voice in the first five minutes. Sounded like me almost immediately.

But it couldn't make the calls I make without thinking. It didn't know when a draft was technically fine but felt off. It had no instinct for which detail to cut and which one to keep because it changes everything. Those decisions come from years of doing the work, and they were the hardest part to teach.

17 rounds of testing later, it got there. And now the thing I never had enough hours for just works. My best thinking shows up in every message, not just the ones I have time to write carefully.

That's the real benefit of teaching AI your judgment. Not speed. Consistency. The version of you that wins when you're focused, showing up even when you're stretched thin.
