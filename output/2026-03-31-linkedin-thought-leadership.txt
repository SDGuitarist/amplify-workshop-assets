After 30 years of building a creative business, here's what nobody tells you about AI.

It doesn't fix your weak spots. It amplifies whatever's already there.

I spent the first six months trying to use AI like everyone says to. Faster emails. Quicker proposals. More output. And it worked, technically. I was producing more. But "more" of what? More generic follow-ups. More templates that could've come from anyone. More volume with less of me in it.

The turn happened when I stopped asking AI to do my job faster and started teaching it how I actually think.

My pricing logic. The way I read a lead and know in thirty seconds whether it's a wedding planner testing three vendors or someone who already decided and just needs to hear the right thing. The follow-up timing I've calibrated over 2,500 gigs.

I've lost gigs to slower responses. Not because the other musician was better. Because they answered first and the client moved on. That's not a sales problem. That's a capacity problem. I knew what to say. I just couldn't say it fast enough to everyone who needed to hear it.

That expertise took years to build. It took about a week to build into a system that runs on it every single day.

The leads didn't improve because AI got smarter. They improved because I finally had the capacity to show up as myself in every interaction. Not just the ones where I had an hour to sit down and think.

That's the difference between using AI and leading it. Using it gives you speed. Leading it gives you consistency. And consistency is what actually wins business.

That's what I'm teaching on April 25. Not AI tips. Not prompt tricks. A way of thinking about your expertise that makes AI work on your terms.
